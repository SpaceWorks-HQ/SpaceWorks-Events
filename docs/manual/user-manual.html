Chapter 1

Welcome to Yuyu

Yuyu is a self-hosted platform for running free events from the first announcement to the last certificate. This chapter explains the moving parts so the rest of the manual makes sense.

What Yuyu is

Yuyu lets an organisation publish events, collect registrations with a custom form, manage capacity and waitlists, email guests with designed messages, issue QR tickets, check people in at the door (even offline) and print their badges, publish a full event website with a programme, and gather feedback and issue certificates afterwards. Everything runs on your own server, so attendee data never leaves your infrastructure.

Always free to attend

Yuyu deliberately has no payments, ticket sales, refunds, or revenue features. Every registration is free for the attendee. If you need paid tickets, Yuyu is not the right tool.

Who this manual is for

Organisers

Owners, admins and co-organisers

Create organisations, publish events and series, build registration forms, manage guests, send emails, and read the numbers. Chapters 3 to 11.

Door staff

Check-in volunteers

Scan tickets, look up guests, print badges, and work offline. Chapter 10 is written for you.

Attendees

People registering

Find events, register, keep your ticket, cancel if plans change, give feedback, and download a certificate. Chapter 12.

Instance admins

Super-admins and developers

Configure email and sign-in for the whole installation, watch delivery and security, and connect other tools through the API. Chapters 13 and 14.

Key terms

Organisation
The workspace that owns events. Members, events, invitations, and files all belong to exactly one organisation. You can belong to several.
Event
A single, standalone happening with its own dates, registration form, event page, and guest list.
Series
A recurring schedule (for example, every Tuesday). Each occurrence is an instance with its own registrations.
Registration
One person’s sign-up (also called an RSVP). In the dashboard it is shown as Going, Waitlist, To approve, or Declined.
Ticket
A private link with a QR code, issued to guests who are going and used at check-in.
Co-organiser
A person given specific permissions on one event without joining the organisation.
Short link
An optional seven-character link such as /aB3x9Kq that always points at an event’s current page.
Super-admin
The single account allowed to configure the whole installation (email, Google sign-in, monitoring).

Chapter 2

Your account

Everything in the dashboard starts with a verified account. This chapter covers signing up and signing in, then walks through the four pages of your account area.

Creating an account

  1. Open the sign-in pageGo to /login and choose the Create account tab. (If the tab is missing, your instance administrator has turned off new accounts.)
  2. Enter your detailsProvide your name, email address, and a password of at least 12 characters.
  3. Verify your inboxYuyu emails you a verification link that works for one hour. Open it and choose Confirm email. Missed it? Use Resend verification email.
  4. Sign inReturn to /login and sign in with your email and password.
Why verification matters

A password account cannot sign in until its email is verified. Verification proves you control the address that attendees will see as the organiser.

Signing in with Google

If your instance administrator has enabled Google sign-in, a Continue with Google button appears on the sign-in page. Google accounts count as verified and rely on Google’s own security policies. If a password account with the same address exists but is not yet verified, verify it first.

Forgot your password?

Use Forgot password? on the sign-in page and then Send reset link. Yuyu emails a one-time link. Setting a new password signs you out on other devices and keeps any authenticator you had set up.

Signing in with two-step verification

If you turned on two-step verification, Yuyu asks Verify it’s you after your password. Enter the six-digit code from your authenticator app or one of your recovery codes, then choose Verify and sign in.

The account area

Open it from the account card at the bottom of the dashboard sidebar (Account settings). The same menu switches between light and dark mode and signs you out. The account area has four tabs: Profile, Registrations, Organisations, and Security. The header shows whether your email is verified and whether two-step verification is on.

Profile

Account›Profile
  • Profile: your display name and photo (JPEG, PNG, or WebP up to 5 MB). Without a photo, Yuyu uses your Google picture, then Gravatar, then your initials.
  • Email: where Yuyu sends your tickets and account emails, with its verification status. Changing the address is not available yet.
  • Appearance: Light, Dark, or System (follows your device). This is saved in the current browser only.

Registrations

Account›Registrations
  • Upcoming and Past list the events you registered for, with their status (Confirmed, Waitlisted, Waiting for approval, Checked in, and so on). Each row has View ticket and Event page.
  • Cancel registration is offered until the event ends, unless you are already checked in.
  • Certificates lists certificates you earned through feedback forms, with View certificate.
  • Download my data gives you a JSON file with your profile, organisations, registrations and answers, feedback, and event roles. Passwords, security codes, and ticket links are never included. You can download it a few times per hour.

Organisations

Account›Organisations
  • Your organisations shows each organisation with your role (Owner, Admin, or Member) and its upcoming events, with Open dashboard.
  • Leave removes only your own membership. If you are the only owner, leaving is blocked. Yuyu does not offer ownership transfer yet, so an only owner stays until the organisation is deleted.
  • Event co-organiser roles lists events where you help as a co-organiser and what you are allowed to do there.
  • Pending invitations lists co-organiser invites sent to your address. Accept them from the link in the invitation email.

A time-based code can only be used once, even across devices. If you are asked for a code twice in a row (for example, signing in and then entering the super-admin area), wait for the next code to appear.

Recommended setup for organisers

Verify your email before the first event.
Turn on two-step verification and store recovery codes.
Use a password manager for the 12+ character password.
Use Sign out everywhere when you change devices.
Chapter 3

Organisations and teams

An organisation is the home for your events and the boundary for who can see what. Set it up once, then bring in the people who help you run things.

Your dashboard home

Dashboard›Home

The first time you sign in, a Get set up checklist walks you through creating your organisation, adding a logo, creating your first event, and publishing it. After that, the home page shows:

  • A reminder bar when people are waiting for approval or events are still drafts, with Review approvals and Finish draft.
  • A Next up card for your next event with Open check-in and Manage event. From two hours before the start it switches to event-day mode: a big check-in count, what is on stage, and recent arrivals.
  • Weekly numbers (upcoming events, sign-ups, check-ins, feedback), Your events (Upcoming, Drafts, Past), recent activity, and your organisations. Events you co-organise for other organisations appear in their own section.

The sidebar on the left can be collapsed to icons. Inside an organisation it shows the organisation switcher, then Events, Series, Members, and Settings, plus View public page.

Create an organisation

Dashboard›New organisation
  1. Choose a nameThis appears on public pages and in emails.
  2. Pick a web addressShort, lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes, unique across the installation. Yuyu tells you whether it is available. It becomes /your-slug and the start of every event address.
  3. Add a description and logoOptional, but both are shown on the public organisation page. Logos are resized and cleaned automatically.

The person who creates the organisation becomes its Owner. Yuyu then opens the new public organisation page.

The organisation workspace

Dashboard›Your organisation

The workspace lists events under Upcoming, Drafts, and Past, with a search box and a small month calendar beside it. Use Create event and New series at the top, and see your series and team in the side panels. A brand-new organisation shows a setup checklist instead.

Inviting members

Dashboard›Organisation›Members
  1. Create a linkIn Invite people, choose Create link and then Copy link. Each link adds one person as a Member, works once, and expires after seven days.
  2. Share the link privatelyThe person opens /join/org/<code> while signed in and chooses Accept invite. Treat the link like a password.
  3. Promote if neededAfter they join, the owner can change their role to Admin from the team list.
  4. Revoke unused linksEvery open link is listed under Open links with a Revoke button.

The team list can be filtered by Everyone, Admins, and Members. Owners can change roles and Remove people. A What each role can do card summarises the roles for your team.

Co-organisers

Sometimes you need help from someone who should not see the whole organisation, such as a volunteer who only runs the door. Co-organisers get specific permissions on one event and do not join the organisation:

PermissionGrants

Attendee invitesMaintain the invite list for invite-only events.

Owners and admins invite co-organisers from the event’s Settings tab (or Manage co-organisers on Overview): enter an email, tick what they can do, and choose Send invite. They receive an email with a link (/join/event-collaborator/<code>) that expires after seven days and must sign in with the invited address to accept. You can Cancel invite before it is accepted, and later Edit permissions or Remove access.

Organisation settings

Dashboard›Organisation›Settings

Edit the logo, name, and description, with a live preview of the public page. The public page address is shown with Copy link and cannot be changed. Owners also see the API access card (Manage API access, Chapter 14) and Delete organisation, which asks you to type the organisation’s name to confirm.

Deletion is permanent

Deleting an organisation removes its events, registrations, invitations, uploaded files, and API clients. There is no undo. Export anything you need first.

Chapter 4

Creating and publishing events

An event in Yuyu is more than a date. It decides who can see it, who can sign up, and how many places there are. This chapter covers creating an event, the event dashboard, and the Publishing section.

Create an event

Dashboard›Organisation›Create event

The Create event dialog has three short steps:

  1. BasicsTitle, About the event (required), and up to 12 tags. Press Enter or a comma after each tag.
  2. ScheduleStart, end, and timezone. The start must be in the future.
  3. Location & platformTick Online event and paste the meeting link, or enter the venue and an optional Google Maps Map link.

New events start as a Draft, visible only to your team, until you make them live.

The event dashboard

Dashboard›Organisation›Event

The header shows the date, title, status, and where and when it happens, with View page, a copy-link button, and Open check-in. Admins see a strip of key numbers (Going, Waitlist, To approve, Checked in). After the event ends, owners and admins also get Download PDF report. Below the header are eight tabs:

Publishing

The Publishing card answers three questions and shows a live summary line at the top, for example “Live. Listed publicly, anyone can sign up · 120 spots · closes 2 hours before.”

Who can see this event?

Draft

Only your team can see it. Nobody can sign up yet.

Live

The event page is public and people can sign up.

Offline

Taken down. The link stops working; guests keep their places.

Who can find it?

Everyone

Listed on your organisation page, Discover, and search.

People with the link

Not listed anywhere. Share the link with who you want.

Invited people only

Only people on the invite list (Guests › Invites) can open the page.

The Approve each sign-up switch makes people ask to join and wait until you accept them in Guests. It works with Everyone (the event stays listed publicly) and with People with the link. Invited people only does not need it, because invited people are confirmed when they sign up.

Sign-ups

  • Limit the number of spots: set how many places there are. When it is full, new sign-ups join the waitlist. Off means unlimited.
  • Close sign-ups: pick one rule. At the end (open until the event ends), Before start (a number of minutes, hours, or days before the start, which moves with the start time), or Set time (an exact date and time, no later than the start). After this, the sign-up button turns off.
  • Show how many are going: guests see the number of sign-ups on the event page.
One switch

Draft, Live, and Offline control both the event page and the sign-up form together. You cannot have a live sign-up form on a page that is not live, which prevents half-ready events going public by accident. Other screens may still call these states Draft, Published, and Hidden.

Sharing: links and short links

The Share card on Overview shows the event’s address with a copy button. Owners and admins can also choose Create short link to get a seven-character link such as yourdomain/aB3x9Kq, which is handy for posters and slides. It keeps working when you change the event’s address and still respects the event’s privacy and invite rules. Regenerate creates a new code and the old short link stops working.

The cover photo editor

Drop, paste, or choose a JPEG, PNG, or WebP image up to 5 MB, then use Edit to open the editor:

  • Shape: Event page banner (16:7, recommended), Wide (16:9), or Square.
  • Position: drag to move, scroll or pinch to zoom, and use the arrow keys to nudge. The photo always fills the frame.
  • Crop tab: rotate left or right, flip, a straighten slider, Show other crops to see where the event page, cards, and social shares cut it, and Reset crop.
  • Adjust tab: looks (Original, Vivid, Warm, Cool, Mono, Soft) and brightness, contrast, and saturation sliders.
  • Where it appears: previews of the event page header, event card, and social share. Choose Apply when you are happy.

Every image is resized, stripped of hidden metadata, and re-encoded for safety on the server.

Address, cloning, embedding, and deletion

Event›Settings
  • Event address changes the end of the URL. Changing it breaks links you already shared (short links keep working).
  • Run it again › Clone event makes a new event with the same information. Guests and invites are not copied.
  • Put it on your website gives you code for a register button or the whole event page. Your administrator must allow your website’s address first.
  • Delete event permanently removes the event and its registrations. You type the event address to confirm.
Chapter 5

Event pages and programmes

Each event can grow from a simple registration page into a full website with a schedule. This lives on Event page › Page & programme. Use Open public page at the top to see the real page in a new tab.

Colour

Choose the accent for the hero band, buttons, and links on the event page. Pick a preset (Ember, Iris, Teal, Ink) or Custom, which opens a picker with a saturation and brightness area, a hue slider, a hex field, and, in browsers that support it, an eyedropper to pick a colour from the screen. A sample register button and link show the result; text and links automatically use a darker shade so they stay readable. Use the default colour goes back to Ember.

Sections

The event page is built from sections. Reorder them with the arrows, switch off anything you don’t need, and open a section to edit its content. Section changes are saved with the bar at the bottom of the screen.

SectionContent
HeroCover, title, tagline, dates, logo, and the register button.
AboutThe description from Details.
HighlightsShort cards with a title, description, and optional icon: “3 tracks”, “Free lunch”, “Hiring booth”.
ProgrammeThe schedule, grouped by track, with links to session pages.
SpeakersName, photo, headline, organisation, biography, and LinkedIn or X links. Each speaker gets a page at /your-org/your-event/speakers/<slug>.

Under Section content, each highlight, speaker, sponsor, resource, and FAQ is saved on its own and has a Visibility of Published, Draft, or Hidden, so you can prepare a sponsor or speaker before revealing them.

Release gate

Page content follows the event’s status. Nothing is public while the event is a draft, and taking the event offline hides the whole page.

Building the programme

Event page›Page & programme›Open schedule

The Programme card summarises your sessions, tracks, and speakers. Open schedule opens the programme schedule page (it needs the Publish and schedule permission).

  1. Create tracksA track is a parallel lane such as Main Hall, Workshop Room, or Online stream. Rename and reorder tracks as needed. Single-track events still need one track.
  2. Add sessionsGive each a title, description, planned start and end, track, type (Keynote, Talk, Panel, Workshop, Fireside chat, Networking, Break, Other, or your own), speakers, and visibility.
  3. Attach resourcesAdd slides or links to a session so they appear next to it.
  4. Check overlapsA Time overlap chip appears when two sessions in the same track overlap. It is allowed, but attendees may need guidance. Overlaps across tracks are normal.

Live delays on the day

When the keynote runs long, set Delay from this session (min). The delay affects only later sessions in the same track, so a late start in the Main Hall does not shift the Workshop Room. Sessions then show both the planned time and the live time (“N min behind”), and the public schedule at /your-org/your-event/schedule updates straight away.

Session and speaker pages

Each published session has its own page with the description, time, track, speakers, and resources. Each speaker page lists their biography and sessions. Both are linked from the programme and can be shared directly.

Chapter 6

Registration forms

Every guest gives their name and email. Add your own questions to collect dietary needs, T-shirt sizes, affiliations, or anything else the event needs.

Editing the form

Event›Event page›Registration form

Name and email are Always collected. Under Your questions, choose Add question at the top or Add another question at the bottom; the picker opens where you asked. A Live preview beside the list updates as you edit. Each question card can be marked Required, moved up or down, duplicated, edited, or deleted (with a short Undo).

Ready-made questions

The Ready-made tab of the picker adds a finished question in one click, which you can edit afterwards: Organisation or company; Job title or role; Phone number; Dietary needs (Vegetarian, Vegan, Gluten-free, Halal, No nuts, plus Other); Accessibility needs; T-shirt size (XS to XXL); How did you hear about this event?; Experience level; I’m happy to appear in event photos; and I agree to the code of conduct (required).

Question settings

  • Help text shown under the question, and an Example answer shown faintly in the empty box.
  • Choices: add them one by one or Paste a list, one per line. Duplicates are merged.
  • Allow an “Other” answer adds an Other choice with a short text box.
  • Limits: fewest and most characters, lowest and highest number, earliest and latest date, or Up to how many choices? for Checkboxes. Leave blank for no limit.

Show this question only if…

Ask a question only when an earlier answer makes it relevant. Pick an Earlier question (a Multiple choice, Dropdown, Checkboxes, or Yes / no question above this one) and tick the answers that should reveal it. Such questions are marked Conditional. Yuyu warns you if a rule breaks, for example after reordering, and offers Move it below or Remove rule.

The server checks every answer against the current form, enforces every setting, and drops answers to questions that were hidden by a rule, so a modified browser cannot bypass required fields or submit options you did not offer. Deleting a question also deletes the answers already given to it.

Tips for good forms

Keep required questions to what you truly need.
Use Multiple choice when there are four options or fewer.
Use Yes / no for photo consent or a code of conduct.
Add Organisation and Job title if you plan to print them on badges.

What attendees see

The register button opens a sign-up dialog in the event’s colour. Signed-in attendees have their name and email prefilled. The same person cannot register twice for the same event or occurrence; Yuyu matches on the signed-in account or the email address.

Chapter 7

Recurring series

A series describes a repeating schedule once. Yuyu then creates individual occurrences, each with its own registrations, so regulars can sign up for the dates they can make.

Create a series

FieldNotes

Managing a series

Dashboard›Organisation›Series
  • Series details edits the shared fields, with Delete series.
  • Invites holds the email list for invite-only series; it applies to every occurrence.
  • Schedule items define a running order that repeats each time, with live delays.
  • Upcoming instances lists the dates, each with View event.
  • Attendees shows the guest list for the next occurrence, with search, a filter for guests or signed-in users, and Download CSV.

Public occurrence pages

Each occurrence has a public page at /your-org/i/<occurrence-id> showing how often it repeats, a Join on <date> box, and View live schedule. The organisation page lists upcoming occurrences alongside standalone events.

Series versus event

Use a series when the format repeats and attendees pick dates. Use a standalone event when it needs its own website, programme, sponsors, emails, feedback form, certificate, co-organisers, or short link. Those features are available on standalone events only.

The guest list

Event›Guests›People
  • Filter with the pills All, Going, Waitlist, To approve, Declined, and Checked in; narrow to Guests only or Signed-in users; and search by name or email. The list shows 25 people per page.
  • Approve or Decline people waiting for approval. The Guests tab shows how many are waiting.

Editing and transferring tickets

For a guest who registered without an account, you can correct their answers or change the name and email to transfer the ticket to someone else. The ticket link, status, and check-in state stay the same. Registrations made by signed-in users can be viewed, but their ownership cannot be transferred.

Waitlist behaviour

When the spots are full, new registrations join the waitlist in order of arrival. When a guest who is going cancels, the person who has waited longest is moved to Going automatically and emailed a ticket (on events where you approve each sign-up, you decide instead). You can also give a spot out of order with Give a spot.

Changing capacity

Raising the number of spots does not move the whole waitlist at once. Use Give a spot for the people you want so they each receive a ticket email.

Invites

Event›Guests›Invites
  1. Add email addressesType an address and choose Add. For events set to Invited people only, only these addresses can register. Matching ignores capital letters.
  2. Invitation emails go outEach address you add is emailed a link to the event’s registration page. Emails are queued and delivered in the background; delivery continues even if you close the tab.
  3. Remove addressesRemoving someone from the list does not cancel a registration they already made. No new invites can be added after the event has ended.

Chapter 9

Emailing your guests

Send reminders, agenda updates, logistics, and thank-yous to people who already registered, without leaving Yuyu or exporting addresses. Everything lives on the Emails tab.

Writing an email

Event›Emails
  1. Pick a starting pointChoose General update, Reminder before the event, Know before you go, Agenda update, or Thank you and feedback. More templates adds Schedule or venue change, Waitlist update, Speaker announcement, Event cancelled, Pending approval update, Live update on the day, Sorry we missed you, and Slides and resources. A template fills in the subject, the message, and a suggested audience. Everything stays editable.
  2. Choose who gets itTick one or more groups (Going, Waitlist, To approve, Declined) and optionally narrow to people who Checked in or Didn’t check in. Yuyu shows how many people will get it: one email per address, even if someone registered twice.
  3. Write itUse the toolbar for bold, italic, headings, lists, quotes, and links. Button inserts a call to action (View your ticket, Open the event page, Share your feedback, or your own). Placeholder inserts details filled in for each recipient (below).
  4. Choose a designPick one of five designs in the preview (below).
  5. Check the previewThe preview is rendered by the server with the real email template and example values, exactly as it will arrive. Switch between Phone and Desktop widths.
  6. Send a test to meThe test goes only to your own verified address with [Test] in the subject. If you are registered and going to the event, the ticket link opens your own ticket; otherwise it opens the event page.
  7. Review and sendChoose Send to N people. The review step shows the recipients and subject; tick I’ve checked the preview and want to email N people to enable sending. A five-second countdown follows with Undo, and leaving the page during the countdown also cancels it.

How delivery works

Each recipient gets their own email, queued and delivered in the background a few at a time, so a large audience takes a few minutes. Ticket links are only added for guests who are going, and anyone who leaves the chosen audience before delivery (for example, by cancelling) is skipped.

Keep it relevant

Only people who registered for this event can be reached, and sends and tests are limited per hour. Yuyu does not yet offer an unsubscribe link, so keep messages about the event itself. Feedback links are never emailed automatically; send them yourself with the Thank you and feedback template.

Chapter 10

Tickets and check-in

On the day, speed and reliability matter more than anything. Yuyu gives you a check-in desk that works on any phone, a mode that works without Wi-Fi, a door station volunteers can use without an account, and badges printed as people arrive.

How tickets work

Every guest who is going gets a private ticket link containing a long random code. The ticket page shows the event, date and time, venue, the guest’s name, and a QR code of that code, with Add to calendar and Save ticket (a picture of the ticket). For in-person events with a map link, the venue opens the map in a new tab. Waitlisted and pending registrations do not have a scannable ticket, which prevents queue-jumping at the door.

When the guest is checked in, an open ticket page tears off its QR stub live and shows You’re checked in with the time, without a reload. Reopening a checked-in ticket shows the torn ticket with no QR code.

Ticket links are the key

Anyone holding a ticket link can check in as that guest. Do not post ticket links publicly or paste them into shared documents.

The Check-in tab

Event›Check-in

The tab shows when doors open and how many people have arrived, with Open check-in to start the desk. It also explains what happens At the door: guests who are going are let in by a scan; waitlisted guests need an override; people waiting for approval, declined, or holding a wrong ticket are blocked with the reason shown. Export attendance downloads who registered and who arrived.

The check-in desk

Available to owners, admins, and co-organisers with the Check-in permission. On a phone or tablet it offers three ways to check someone in:

Scan QR

Point the camera at the guest’s ticket. The browser asks for camera permission the first time. The code holds only the secret ticket link.

Manual code

Paste the ticket URL or code if the screen is cracked or the camera is unavailable.

Search attendees

Search by name or email and check in directly from the results.

Each check-in asks you to Confirm (press Enter). Already checked-in tickets are flagged so a shared screenshot cannot be reused. Switch on Override waitlist to let waitlisted guests in at the door; people waiting for approval and declined registrations stay blocked. Recent check-ins lists arrivals with an Undo for mistakes.

Working offline

  1. Install the check-in appWhere the browser offers it, use Install check-in app to add the desk to the device’s home screen.
  2. Make available offlineBefore you lose connectivity, save the guest list to the device. It is stored only in that browser and expires after 24 hours; use Refresh roster to update it.
  3. Check in as usualScans and searches work against the saved list. Each check-in is queued on the device.
  4. Sync when back onlineThe queue uploads automatically when a connection returns, or use Sync. Every check-in keeps its original time.

The saved list contains personal data. Use Remove offline data after the event (sync first).

The door station (no account needed)

/your-org/your-event/check-in

For volunteers who should not have dashboard access, an admin turns on the Door station on the Check-in tab and shares the station link (or its QR code) and the 8-digit PIN.

  • Turn on station, Show PIN, Copy link, New PIN, and Turn off station are for organisation admins only. A new PIN immediately locks out every device that used the old one.
  • The station opens from 24 hours before the event starts until 1 hour after it ends.
  • Volunteers can scan, search, check in, and undo. The station works online only; it cannot save the guest list offline or download the full guest export.
  • Turn the station off when the event is over.

Printing badges

Switch on ID card printing on the check-in desk. From then on, every check-in (scan, manual code, or search) opens a quick print sheet showing that guest’s real badge with Print badge (press Enter), Skip, and Edit design. Print at 100% (actual size), without headers or footers.

Check-in history

Every check-in and undo, online or offline, is recorded with who did it and when. Nothing is overwritten, so you can reconstruct the day if a question comes up.

Chapter 11

Feedback, certificates and analytics

Ask attendees how it went, reward the people who showed up with a certificate, and see the whole event in numbers.

Set up a feedback form

Event›Feedback
  1. Start from a template or blankUse a template offers Quick pulse (2 questions), Event review (5), and Session & speaker review (4). A template closes the form so you can review it.
  2. Tailor the questionsEdit the title and thank-you message, and add, remove, or reorder questions. Answer types are Short answer, Long answer, Dropdown, Pick several, Pick one, Yes / no, and Number.
  3. Decide about certificatesTurn on the Certificate switch to offer a JPEG certificate after someone submits, then Design certificate. See below for how this changes privacy.
  4. Open the formSwitch the form to Open. It lives at /your-org/your-event/feedback.
  5. Share the linkYuyu never emails the link on its own. Use Copy feedback link, or send it with the Thank you and feedback email template (Chapter 9).

Anonymous or verified?

Certificates off

Anonymous feedback

No email or registration identity is collected. People can respond more than once. Best for honest, low-friction feedback.

Certificates on

Verified feedback

The respondent enters the email they registered with, which must match a guest who is going. After sending they can choose Download certificate (JPG).

Questions can change safely

Each response stores a snapshot of the question text and type at the time it was submitted. Editing or deleting a question later never alters what earlier respondents said.

Reading responses

Responses summarises each question without names: ratings such as Excellent to Poor show the most common answer, and number scales show the average. Show individual responses lists the most recent 250, and Download CSV exports them all. Exports are audited.

The certificate designer

  1. Choose a backgroundUpload background takes a JPEG, PNG, or WebP up to 5 MB in landscape or portrait, or keep Yuyu’s default certificate. Leave out any personal data.
  2. Add layersBuild the certificate from up to 20 layers. Add text for words and Add image for logos, signatures, or seals (transparency is kept).
  3. Insert valuesText layers can include values that change per certificate: Attendee name, Event title, Event date, Organisation, and Issue date. Dates use the event’s timezone.
  4. Style the textFont (Classic serif, Clean sans, Monospace), size, colour, letter spacing, alignment, bold, italic, and uppercase. Line breaks are kept and long text shrinks to fit its box.
  5. ArrangeDrag layers (they snap to the centre), resize them, nudge with the arrow keys, or type exact positions. Bring layers forward or send them back, duplicate, or delete. Yuyu warns you if no layer shows the attendee’s name.
  6. Preview and saveEnter a long Preview attendee name and choose Generate exact preview; the server renders it exactly as attendees will receive it. Then save the feedback settings.

Each response keeps the design that was active when it was submitted, so redesigning later only affects new certificates.

What a certificate proves

Eligibility is checked by matching the email of a confirmed registration, not by proving control of that inbox. For certificates that carry real-world value, pair them with a verified attendee list of your own.

Analytics

Event›Analytics

Live numbers while the event is coming up; final numbers once it has ended.

  • Key numbers: Going (and places left), Waitlist, Waiting for approval (with Review), and Turnout.
  • From sign-up to thank-you: a funnel of Responded (everyone who registered or asked to join), Going, Checked in, and Gave feedback. Each bar is measured against everyone who responded, and each step shows its share of the previous one, never over 100%. Later steps fill in after the event.
  • Where every response stands: the split between Going, Waitlist, Waiting for approval, and Declined.
  • When people signed up: sign-ups per day in the event’s timezone, with the total, the daily average, and the busiest day.

The event report

Once the event has ended, owners and admins can choose Download report (PDF) on the Analytics tab or Download PDF report in the header. The report opens with the event itself and covers attendance, sign-ups over time, arrivals at the door, the programme, feedback, registration answers, and emails and team, and says when it was generated. It contains counts only: no guest is named.

For attendees

You do not need an account to attend a Yuyu event. This chapter is written for the people who register, and organisers may want to share it.

Finding an event

  • Browse /discover for upcoming public events. Search by title, tag, or organiser, filter by dates (This week, This month, or your own range), and sort by Upcoming or Popular.
  • An organisation’s page (/their-name) lists everything they have published.
  • Some events are shared by private link, short link, or invitation only and will not appear in search.

Registering

  1. Open the event pageRead the details, programme, venue directions, and FAQ.
  2. Choose the register buttonIt reads Register — it’s free, Join the waitlist when the event is full, or Request to join when the organiser approves each sign-up.

Your ticket

Feedback and certificates

Chapter 13

Instance administration

One account per installation is designated as the super-admin. It configures services for everyone and watches over delivery, security, and storage. This chapter is a tour; deployment itself is covered in the technical documentation.

Getting in

  1. Be the configured accountOnly the verified email set as the instance’s super-admin can enter /super-admin. Everyone else sees a normal “not found” page.
  2. Turn on two-step verificationFrom Account › Security. The super-admin area is not available without an authenticator.
  3. Pass the step-up checkYuyu asks you to Verify super-admin access with a fresh six-digit code. The check lasts 10 minutes, after which you are asked again.

The super-admin area

The super-admin area has its own top bar instead of the dashboard sidebar, with Back to dashboard and your account menu on the right and a row of sections below. Pages are read-only views unless noted, and never show passwords, tokens, registration answers, or message bodies.

Instance settings

SMTP
Sender address, service or host, port, username, and password, with options for implicit TLS or a private relay. Values saved here override anything configured on the server. The password is write-only: it is encrypted and never shown again; leave the field blank to keep it.
Google SSO

How email delivery works

Yuyu never sends email while an attendee is waiting. Every message is queued and a background worker sends it within about a minute, retrying on temporary failures. The Operations and Email sections show the queue; if it looks stale, check the server and the SMTP settings. Messages that contain private links are deleted from the queue once delivered.

Notes for whoever runs the server

  • Public address: set NEXT_PUBLIC_BASE_URL to the site’s public address (the same as AUTH_URL). Emails use it for every link and image; without it, delivered emails leave out the event cover, organisation logo, and site links, even though the dashboard preview looks right. Changing it needs a rebuild.
  • Redis: REDIS_URL is required in production. Besides rate limits, it carries live check-in updates to tickets, so run more than one replica only with a shared Redis.
  • Reverse proxy: the live ticket update stream must not be buffered (for nginx, proxy_buffering off) and must allow idle reads of at least 30 seconds. A buffering proxy delays the ticket tear until the page’s regular status check.
  • Embeds: list the websites allowed to embed event pages in ALLOWED_EMBED_ORIGINS; leave it blank to block embedding.
Keep secrets out of chat

SMTP passwords, Google secrets, and server keys should only ever be typed into the Settings form or a secrets manager. If one is pasted somewhere public, rotate it.

Chapter 14

Connecting other tools (API)

Organisation owners can let an external system read event details and a minimal attendance list. The API is deliberately small and privacy-preserving.

Create an API client

Dashboard›Organisation›Settings›Manage API access
  1. Sign in recentlyChanges to API access need a recent sign-in. If Yuyu asks, sign out and in again first.
  2. Name the clientUnder Create API client, give an application name such as “Badge printer” or “Community dashboard”.
  3. Choose scopesSee the table. Grant only what the tool needs.
  4. Copy the credentialGive the credential a label and an optional expiry, then Create client. The secret is shown once. Copy it into the consuming tool’s secret store and choose I have stored it.

To rotate, use Create rotation credential, switch the tool over, check that it works, then Revoke the old one. Disable client stops all its credentials immediately.

Scopes

ScopeAllows
events:readList and read the organisation’s standalone events (title, dates, status, privacy, and similar).
participants:readRead confirmed participants as display name and registration time only. May filter by checked in or not.
participants:attendance:readTogether with the scope above, include the check-in time.

Participant responses never include email addresses, form answers, user IDs, or ticket codes.

Calling the API

GET /api/v1/events?limit=50
GET /api/v1/events/<eventId>
GET /api/v1/events/<eventId>/participants?attendance=checked_in&include=attendance

Authorization: Bearer <credential>

Collections are paginated with a nextCursor; pass it back unchanged to get the next page. Errors share one shape with a code such as INVALID_CREDENTIAL, INSUFFICIENT_SCOPE, RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND, or RATE_LIMITED. A developer reference sits below the client list, and full details are in the API reference in the technical documentation.

Troubleshooting and FAQ

Most problems have a one-line answer. Scan the question column, then follow the fix.

ProblemLikely cause and fix
I can’t sign in; it says to verify my email.Open the verification link from the sign-up email (it lasts one hour) or use Resend verification email.
My event isn’t in Discover or search.Check Publishing: it must be Live and found by Everyone. Events for people with the link or invited people only are never listed.
Attendees say registration is closed.Look at Close sign-ups in Publishing, check the spots, and confirm the event hasn’t ended.
Someone can’t register for an invite-only event.Their email must be on Guests › Invites. Add it, then ask them to try again.
A registration says “already registered”.That email or account already has a registration, possibly on the waitlist. Search for it on Guests.
The short link stopped working.Someone chose Regenerate, or the event is not live. Copy the current short link from the Share card on Overview.
A ticket won’t scan.Only guests who are going have tickets. Check the status; give a spot from the waitlist or use Override waitlist if appropriate. Also try Manual code.
The camera doesn’t open on the check-in desk.Allow camera access in the browser’s site settings. The page must be served over HTTPS.
The door station says it can’t be opened.The station is only available from 24 hours before the start until 1 hour after the end, and an admin may have turned it off.

Confirmation emails aren’t arriving.Ask attendees to check spam first. Then the super-admin should check the Email and Operations sections for failed messages and verify the SMTP settings.
I sent an email by mistake.Choose Undo during the five-second countdown. Once delivery starts, emails can’t be recalled.
I removed the wrong guest.Choose Undo in the confirmation message straight away. After that, add them again with Add guest.

I can’t leave an organisation.You are its only owner. Ownership can’t be transferred yet, so the only owner can leave only by deleting the organisation.
I lost my authenticator phone.Sign in with a recovery code, then turn two-step verification off and on again with the new device. If you have no codes, the super-admin cannot bypass it; contact your administrator.

The super-admin page keeps asking for a code.The step-up lasts 10 minutes. Wait for a fresh code; a code can’t be reused from the sign-in prompt.
Can I sell tickets?No. Yuyu is for free events only.
